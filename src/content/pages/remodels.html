Remodel Plumbing

A remodel is the best chance you'll get to fix what's behind the walls. Once the drywall is open and the floor is up, the plumbing is right there: old supply lines, tired drains, valves nobody has turned in twenty years. {{ client.name }} plans and installs remodel plumbing so the finished room works as well as it looks, and keeps working long after the paint dries.

Built on a New Construction Background

Our founder came up through new construction, remodeling, and service plumbing. That mix matters on a remodel. New construction teaches you how a plumbing system should be laid out from scratch. Remodeling teaches you how to work around what's already there. Service plumbing teaches you what fails years later, and why. We bring all three to your project.

Kitchen Remodels

Kitchens pack a lot of plumbing into a small space. A new layout can mean moving the sink, adding an island prep sink, running a line for a refrigerator or pot filler, or adding gas for a new range. We handle:

  • Relocating sink drains and supply lines to match a new layout
  • Dishwasher, refrigerator, and ice maker connections
  • Garbage disposal installation
  • Gas piping for ranges and cooktops
  • Faucets, including pull-down and pot filler models
  • Water filtration at the kitchen sink

Bathroom Remodels

A bathroom remodel lives or dies on the plumbing behind the tile. A shower valve set at the wrong depth or a drain without proper slope is expensive to fix once the finish work is done. We take care of:

  • Shower and tub valves, including custom shower layouts
  • Drain relocation for new tub, shower, and vanity positions
  • Toilet relocation and new toilet installation
  • Vanity and sink rough-in and faucet installation
  • Converting a tub to a walk-in shower

Additions and Basement Finishes

Adding a bathroom, a laundry room, or a wet bar means tying new lines into your existing system. We plan the drain, waste, and vent layout, size the supply lines, and make sure the new fixtures have the pressure and flow they need. If a new bathroom sits below the main sewer line, a sewage ejector pump may be part of the plan, and we can install that too.

From Rough-In to Fixtures

Remodel plumbing happens in two main stages. Rough-in is everything that goes inside the walls and floors before they're closed: drains, vents, supply lines, valves, and gas piping. Finish is setting fixtures once the walls, tile, and cabinets are in: toilets, faucets, shower trim, and appliance connections. We handle both, so the person who planned the pipes is the one who connects the fixtures.

Repipes

If your home still has aging galvanized steel or other piping near the end of its life, a remodel is the right time to think about a repipe. With the walls already open, replacing old supply lines costs less and disrupts less than doing it later as a separate job. Signs your pipes may be due include low pressure throughout the house, discolored water, frequent pinhole leaks, and visible corrosion at fittings. We'll look at what you have and give you an honest read on whether a full repipe, a partial one, or no repipe at all makes sense.

Working With You or Your Contractor

Some homeowners run their own remodel and hire each trade directly. Others have a general contractor managing the project. We work either way. If you're running the job, we'll help you sequence the plumbing so it lines up with demolition, framing, and finish work. If you have a contractor, we'll coordinate with them on scheduling and layout so the plumbing isn't the thing holding up the tile setter.

Planning Comes First

Thorough plumbing starts before anything gets cut. We walk the space with you, listen to what you want the finished room to do, look at the existing plumbing, and talk through the options. Sometimes the layout you have in mind works as drawn. Sometimes a small change saves real money. Either way, you'll know the plan before work starts, and the decision is always yours.
