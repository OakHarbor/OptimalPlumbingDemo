Tank Water Heaters

A traditional tank water heater stores a set amount of hot water, usually somewhere between 30 and 80 gallons in a typical home, and keeps it hot until you need it. Tank heaters run on gas or electricity.

  • Pros: Lower upfront cost, simple design, familiar to most homeowners, and a straightforward swap when an old tank fails.
  • Cons: Once the stored hot water is used up, you wait for the tank to recover. They also spend energy keeping water hot around the clock, and sediment builds up in the bottom of the tank over time.

Tankless Water Heaters

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a hot tap. Water passes through a heat exchanger and comes out hot on demand, with no storage tank.

  • Pros: Continuous hot water as long as you stay within the unit's flow capacity, a much smaller footprint, and no energy spent keeping a tank hot overnight.
  • Cons: Higher upfront cost, and switching from a tank often means upgrading the gas line, venting, or electrical service. Tankless units also need periodic descaling, especially with hard water.

Neither type is right for every house. The better choice depends on how your household uses hot water, what your home can support, and what you want to spend now versus over the life of the unit.

Signs Your Water Heater Is Failing

Water heaters usually give some warning before they quit. Call a plumber if you notice:

  • Water pooling around the base. A leaking tank is often a sign the tank itself has corroded, and that doesn't get better.
  • Rust-colored hot water. Discoloration only on the hot side can point to corrosion inside the tank.
  • Popping, rumbling, or banging. Sediment on the bottom of the tank traps water that boils and pops as the burner fires.
  • Running out of hot water faster than you used to. Sediment takes up space, or a heating element or burner may not be doing its job.
  • Water that's never quite hot enough. This can be a thermostat, an element, a gas valve, or a sizing problem.
  • Age. Tank water heaters don't last forever. If yours is well into its second decade and showing any of the signs above, it's worth planning ahead rather than waiting for a flood.

If the tank is actively leaking, shut off the cold water supply valve at the top of the heater and turn off the gas or the breaker. Then call us.

Sizing to How Your Household Uses Hot Water

A water heater that's too small leaves someone taking a cold shower. One that's too big costs more to buy and more to run. Sizing is about more than the number of bedrooms. We ask questions like:

  • How many people shower in the morning, and do they overlap?
  • Do you have a large soaking tub that needs a lot of hot water at once?
  • Do the dishwasher and washing machine often run while someone is showering?
  • Are you planning a remodel or addition that will add fixtures?

For a tank, that tells us the right capacity and recovery rate. For tankless, it tells us the flow rate the unit needs to handle at peak demand. Getting this right up front is part of doing the job once.

Repair or Replace? An Honest Answer

Not every water heater problem means a new water heater. A bad thermostat, a failed heating element, a worn thermocouple, or a faulty gas valve can often be repaired for a fraction of the cost of replacement. A leaking tank, on the other hand, can't be patched. The tank has to go.

When we diagnose your water heater, we'll tell you what we found, what the repair would involve, and whether the unit is worth putting money into. If a repair makes sense, we'll say so. If the heater is near the end of its life and a repair would only buy a few months, we'll say that too. From there, it's your house, your budget, and your decision. Water Heater Maintenance

Flushing a tank water heater to clear sediment helps it run more efficiently and can extend its life. Testing the temperature and pressure relief valve makes sure an important safety device still works. Tankless units benefit from regular descaling. Boilers and Hydronic Heating

Some homes heat with a boiler instead of a furnace, circulating hot water through baseboards, radiators, or in-floor tubing. We also service boilers and hydronic systems, including circulator pumps, expansion tanks, and leaks in the heating loop. If your home has one, mention it when you call.
